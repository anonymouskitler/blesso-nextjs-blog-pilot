A small team does not need a long procurement process to choose an AI interview platform, but it does need good questions. These seven cover the rubric, the interview itself, the evidence and the data. They use [HappierHire](https://www.happierhire.ai/) as the example. Its features below are vendor descriptions from its [official site](https://www.happierhire.ai/), not independently verified results, so confirm them yourself.

## 1. Can I build a rubric for this specific role?

A generic scorecard produces generic interviews. HappierHire says you paste a job description, get a generated rubric, and can customize it for each role. Whatever you evaluate, check that you can edit what "strong" means before any candidate is interviewed.

## 2. How does it handle follow-up questions?

A vague answer deserves a probing question. HappierHire describes an interviewer that adapts to what the candidate says and asks follow-ups. Ask to see a sample, and check that follow-ups stay tied to your rubric, since varied conversations are harder to compare.

## 3. What is the candidate experience?

Candidates should know what they are walking into. HappierHire says candidates are told up front that they are interviewing with an AI, receive a unique link, and can interview at any time. Invitations can be sent individually or by CSV. Try the candidate side yourself, then ask a few real candidates how it felt.

## 4. What evidence sits behind a score?

A score alone is hard to challenge. HappierHire describes structured scores with timestamped evidence, so reviewers can jump to the moment behind a rating. Open several and ask whether the evidence really supports the number.

## 5. Where do humans review and decide?

The tool should prepare evidence, not make the call. HappierHire describes side-by-side candidate comparison and flags for suspicious answers. Treat a flag as a reason to watch the clip, not a verdict. Decide in advance who reviews flags and what they record.

## 6. What do you ask about data handling?

Interview recordings are personal data. Rather than assume anything, put these to every vendor in writing:

- What is stored, where, and for how long?
- Can candidate data be deleted on request?
- Is interview data used to train models?
- What security evidence can you see, and is it current?
- Which notices or consents might your team need? Ask your legal or HR adviser.

HappierHire publishes security statements on its site. Request the supporting documentation instead of relying on a badge.

## 7. How will you run a pilot?

Evaluate with evidence from your own hiring, not a demo.

- Choose one role and write its rubric first.
- Run a small group of candidates through the platform.
- Have two reviewers score independently, then compare.
- Check the timestamped evidence behind several scores.
- Review every flag by hand and note your conclusion.
- Collect candidate feedback on clarity and comfort.

## The short checklist

Rubric you can edit. Follow-ups that stay on topic. Candidates who are informed. Scores you can trace. Humans who decide. Data answers in writing. A pilot you can judge.

If a platform cannot support those, keep looking. If it can, run the pilot and let your reviewers' judgment, not the pitch, make the case.
